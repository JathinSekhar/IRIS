# Demo walkthrough (illustrative only)

> **Illustrative only. Not used for ML training, validation or evaluation, and not evidence that IRIS works.** The three people are fictional demo accounts written by hand (see `docs/DEMO_ACCOUNTS.md`). No model was run to produce this table.

## The V1 design this table follows

```
rules + check-in floor  ->  AI ranks the queue  ->  a person reviews EVERY case  ->  the person decides
```

- The rule layer and the check-in safety floor can only raise urgency. The highest layer wins.
- The AI model only decides the **order** in which people read cases. It can raise priority. It never clears a case as Low.
- Every case goes to a human reviewer. The urgency shown below is a **provisional priority**, not a decision.

## How to read this

- **Ananya** has nothing raising her priority, so her provisional priority is Low. A person still looks at it: the model is not allowed to clear her, and with no model loaded the fail-safe applies in any case.
- **Arjun** is High because of his check-in: the C-SSRS answers set a High floor. The rule layer separately flags his words at *review* level (a Medium floor), which alone would not reach High. No model can lower either layer.
- **Rohit** shows a gap worth a decision. His check-in baseline is **medium** (PHQ-9 12, GAD-7 11), but only the self-harm answers feed the floor that message triage receives, and his tier is 0. So his provisional priority is Low. In the V1 design a person still reads it and the AI ranking could raise it, but whether a medium baseline from the questionnaire scores should also raise the floor is **open for the project owner and a clinician**.
