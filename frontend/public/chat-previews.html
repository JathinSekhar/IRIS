A normal messenger. Replies are short; the tips are tucked into one tap. The emergency strip is pinned once at the top instead of repeated under every reply. Helplines become tap-to-call buttons.

GoodFamiliar, least work to build, short replies, easy on small phones.

GoodTurns text into things you can do; chat never gets long; helplines always on screen.

What every option includes (this fixes the problems you found)

  • Helplines you can tap to call, and a calm "Need help now? Call 112" strip that is pinned once, not repeated in every reply.
  • Shorter replies, with no repeated "Thank you for telling us how you are feeling…" paragraph every time.
  • "A person will read this" shown on each message, so nobody waits wondering. (It would need one small backend field.)
  • Better replies for good news. "My exams went well" gets a warm "That is lovely to hear" and no tips.
  • Helplines for "I can't take it anymore" and similar phrases that you tested and that currently get tips only.
  • A typing indicator, auto-scroll, and a dark mode for night use.
My recommendation: A for the base (fast to build, familiar), with B's helpline card for any message that triggers the safety layer. If you want something more ambitious for the capstone demo, C looks the most impressive on a laptop. I would not pick D or E for v1: D needs more flows, and E is unusual for a "chat" product.
